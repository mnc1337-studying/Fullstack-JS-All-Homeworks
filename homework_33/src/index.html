Amazing Nature Facts

Three-hearted octopus

An octopus has three hearts: two pump blood through the gills, and one circulates it to the rest of the body.

Everlasting honey

Honey almost never spoils. Archaeologists have found pots of edible honey in ancient Egyptian tombs.

The wood wide web

Trees can exchange nutrients and chemical signals through underground networks of fungi connecting their roots.

Speedy bamboo

Some bamboo species can grow almost a meter in a single day.

Blue whale

The blue whale is the largest animal known to have ever lived. It can reach about 30 meters in length and weigh over 150 tonnes.

Lightning

Lightning strikes the Earth roughly 100 times every second.

Amazon rainforest

The Amazon is home to about one in ten of all known species on our planet.

Slow but patient

By slowing down their heart rate, sloths can hold their breath underwater for up to 40 minutes.

Icy desert

Antarctica is the largest desert on Earth, because it receives very little precipitation.

Sun followers

Young sunflowers turn their heads to follow the Sun across the sky.

Clever crows

Crows can recognize individual human faces and remember people who treated them badly, sometimes for years.

Why ice floats

Water is densest at about 4 °C. Ice is lighter than liquid water, so lakes freeze from the top down, and fish can survive the winter below the surface.

Tardigrades

These microscopic animals have survived exposure to the vacuum of outer space.

Giant sequoias

Giant sequoias are the largest trees on Earth by volume, and some of them have lived for more than 3,000 years.

Half-asleep dolphins

Dolphins sleep with only one half of the brain at a time, so they can keep swimming and surfacing to breathe.
