Nature statements

Trees in a forest don't just grow side by side — they communicate and support one another through an underground fungal network nicknamed the "Wood Wide Web." Through this network, older "mother trees" transfer sugar, water, and nutrients to young saplings growing in the shade. Furthermore, if a tree is attacked by pests, it sends a chemical warning signal to its neighbors so they can proactively produce defensive toxins.

The world ocean is home to the only scientifically proven "immortal" creature on Earth — a tiny jellyfish called Turritopsis dohrnii. When injured, aging, or starving, it can completely reset its life cycle, transforming back into a youthful polyp from which new jellyfish grow. Theoretically, this rejuvenation process can go on indefinitely. Additionally, it is ocean phytoplankton — not tropical rainforests — that produces between 50% and 85% of all the oxygen we breathe on Earth.

Deep beneath the Chihuahuan Desert in Mexico lies the Cave of the Crystals, home to some of the largest natural selenite crystals ever found, with individual beams reaching over 30 feet in length and weighing up to 55 tons. These giant pillars grew over hundreds of thousands of years in a unique environment flooded with mineral-rich water heated by magma below. With temperatures inside reaching up to 136°F (58°C) and near 100% humidity, humans can only survive inside for a few minutes without specialized protective suits.
